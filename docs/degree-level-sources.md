# Where the degree-level expectations come from

The skills treat Bachelor's and Master's proposals with one skeleton: the degree level is recorded only in the subtitle wording (`structure.json` → `subtitle.wordings`), and it changes behavior only as graded judgement — the guidelines' Degree Level section and the tailoring it drives in five skills — never as structure. This page records the evidence base for that design, from a literature survey on 2026-09-01 across six source families: European qualification frameworks, the German HQR, statutory qualification descriptors (SE/UK/NL/NO/AU), computing curricula and department documents, peer-reviewed assessment literature, and methods textbooks. It exists so that "why doesn't the checker branch on level?" gets a citation instead of a shrug.

The survey's headline result carries the design: **every source family that describes both levels describes the same document and the same process, with a higher bar** — the difference between the two thesis types is graded, not structural. Where a source draws the grade, it draws it on a small set of dimensions, listed below.

## The dimensions and their sources

### Novelty and contribution

- **Bachelor:** competent application or synthesis of established knowledge; originality is permitted but not required. The GI recommendations state that knowledge creation occurs at most incipiently (*ansatzweise*) in Bachelor theses (Gesellschaft für Informatik, Empfehlungen für Bachelor- und Masterprogramme im Studienfach Informatik, 2016). Berndtsson et al. concede the undergraduate contribution may be relatively small — limited time, research skills still being learned — while insisting a real contribution stays possible (*Thesis Projects*, Springer, 2nd ed. 2008, ch. 11).
- **Master:** an original contribution expected outright. The Dublin second-cycle descriptor asks for originality in developing and applying ideas within a research context (Joint Quality Initiative, 2004; QF-EHEA, Bergen 2005); the Swedish Higher Education Ordinance demands contribution to the formation of knowledge by statute (1993:100, Annex 2); Euro-Inf's second-cycle criteria and the AQF level-9 descriptors concur (EQANIE 2017; AQF 2nd ed. 2013).
- **Convergence:** five independent source families — the strongest-converged claim in the corpus. The AQF makes the split structural: its pass-Bachelor level 7 carries no research outcomes at all; research first appears at Honours (level 8).

### Research-question origin

- **Bachelor:** questions derived from a given context — the HQR's Bachelor level says students *leiten Forschungsfragen ab* (derive research questions); a sound literature search is the level's core research competence (HQR, KMK/HRK 2017).
- **Master:** questions self-conceived — the HQR's Master level says *entwerfen Forschungsfragen* (conceive/design research questions), gap-driven and ideally testable. The MEDINE2 European consensus survey places formulating one's own research problem at cycle 2 (Marz et al., Perspectives on Medical Education 2(4), 2013); KTH's second-cycle degree project makes the student-authored, examiner-approved proposal the entry gate (DA231X syllabus).
- **Convergence:** four families. The HQR verb shift is the crispest single formulation.

### Methodological rigor

- **Bachelor:** apply an established method accurately; name it and show it will be applied correctly (QAA FHEQ level 6, 2024; Euro-Inf first cycle, 2017).
- **Master:** select among alternative methods and justify the choice; critique methodologies; act under incomplete information (Dublin making-judgements strand, 2004; HQR 2017; QAA FHEQ level 7, 2024; Euro-Inf second cycle, 2017).
- **Convergence:** four unrelated frameworks escalate the same verbs identically. The KTH grading rubrics (below) operationalize exactly this row.

### Independence

- **Bachelor:** work under guidance (*unter Anleitung*, HQR 2017); supervisor scaffolding — provided datasets, predefined milestones — is legitimate at this level.
- **Master:** largely self-directed (*weitgehend selbstgesteuert bzw. autonom*, HQR 2017); the supervisor advises rather than directs. Corroborated by the NLQF level descriptors (referencing report 2020), the QAA Scotland "Mastersness" paper (2013), and two empirical studies of supervision practice (Olsson & Hallberg, Journal of Academic Writing 8(1), 2018; Tuononen & Parpala, Studies in Educational Evaluation 70, 2021).
- **Convergence:** five families including the peer-reviewed one.

### Literature depth

- **Bachelor:** the established literature of the field plus selected current papers; textbook and survey anchors acceptable (EQF level 6, Council Recommendation 2017/C 189/03; HQR 2017).
- **Master:** the current research frontier of the specialty, critically evaluated, with the gap derived from the critique (EQF level 7; QAA FHEQ level 7, 2024; KTH DA231X). Berndtsson et al. order the source hierarchy the same way: textbooks teach established knowledge and omit the latest findings; theses and papers carry the frontier (2008, ch. 6).
- **Convergence:** four families.

### Scope and problem complexity

- **Bachelor:** a well-bounded problem inside the studied field, answerable with the field's known solution repertoire and data the student can realistically gather.
- **Master:** problems in new or unfamiliar environments and broader, possibly multidisciplinary contexts; judgements with incomplete or limited information (Dublin applying-knowledge strand, 2004; Swedish Higher Education Ordinance, Annex 2; AQF level 9; Euro-Inf 2017).
- **Convergence:** four families.

### Workload envelope

- **Bachelor:** Germany 6–12 ECTS (KMK Strukturvorgaben 2010; Musterrechtsverordnung 2024, § 8), typically 3–4 months processing time; Sweden ≥ 15 credits (Higher Education Ordinance).
- **Master:** Germany 15–30 ECTS, typically 6 months; Sweden ≥ 30 credits. Berndtsson et al. calibrate the M.Sc. thesis at about half a year of research (2008, ch. 6).
- **Convergence:** the Master floor sits at or above the Bachelor ceiling everywhere the envelope is codified — roughly a factor of two throughout. Thesis credits remain a programme decision: European frameworks fix cycle totals only, so any tooling must treat thesis credits and duration as workspace configuration, not a constant.

## The rubric that makes it operational

The single strongest artifact the survey found is KTH's rector's decision V-2015-0144, whose appendices publish side-by-side grading criteria for first-cycle and second-cycle degree projects: **the same goal rows, with escalated pass criteria**. The method row requires the chosen method *motiverad* (motivated) at first cycle, but *väl motiverad* and additionally evaluated against alternative methods at second cycle. The independence row requires the task handled *till stor del självständigt* (to a large extent independently) at first cycle, *självständigt och systematiskt* — no qualifier, plus systematicity — at second. This is the graded-not-structural finding expressed as an assessment instrument, from a source family (institutional rubrics) independent of the frameworks above. German CS-faculty grading forms that publish both levels show the same shape: identical rubric rows, with structural add-ons (a Master colloquium area) rather than reworded criteria.

## What the grade looks like in a proposal

No framework source addresses thesis proposals directly — the descriptors define degree outcomes, and most university proposal guides the survey checked apply identical proposal requirements to both levels. The implications below are derivations, offered as review guidance rather than checkable structure:

- **Contribution close.** A Bachelor proposal may legitimately promise to apply or evaluate something competently in a named setting; a Master proposal should name what will be new and for whom. Demanding a novelty claim from a Bachelor proposal is a level error in the same way accepting its absence from a Master proposal is.
- **Research questions.** At Bachelor level, visibly deriving the questions from a given topic is the level-appropriate move; at Master level the gap should be argued from the literature and the questions self-formulated.
- **Methodology.** The Bachelor bar is naming one established method and showing it will be applied correctly; the Master bar adds comparing candidates, justifying the winner, and a limitations plan.
- **State of the art.** A descriptive survey of key sources can pass at Bachelor level; at Master level the critique of the literature is what produces the gap.
- **Timeline and scope.** Sized from the programme's credit envelope: a Bachelor plan carrying a Master-scale evaluation is over-scoped, and vice versa. A Master plan showing self-set milestones and named risks is itself evidence of the expected autonomy.

## Caveats the survey recorded

- **Graded, not categorical.** Olsson and Hallberg (2018) find the structure and challenge types similar at both levels, with only the bar for independence and critical reflection rising. One German Prüfungsordnung the survey read states its Bachelor and Master thesis clauses in nearly identical words, the level carried by a single added *selbständig*, the purpose clause, and the credit budget. Incipient novelty in a Bachelor proposal is permissible — just never required.
- **Proposal-level differentiation is institutionally rare.** Direct evidence of level-graded proposal requirements surfaced at two institutions (one chair's page-count calibration; KTH's syllabus pair); several others checked publish one set of proposal requirements for both levels. Single-institution numbers (proposal pages, interview counts, thesis page ranges) are illustrations, not rules.
- **Jurisdictional flavor differs.** Sweden demands the Master contribution by statute; Germany differentiates chiefly through workload plus autonomy verbs; the UK through method-critique verbs; Australia removes research from the pass-Bachelor level entirely. The direction converges, the emphasis does not — which is precisely why level-dependent rules belong in a workspace, not in the shipped defaults.
- **Documented bars are not measured bars.** Tuononen and Parpala (2021) find thesis grades relate only weakly to competences and learning processes at either level; the framework deltas describe expectations, not what grading reliably rewards.
- **The standard CS thesis textbook does not differentiate.** Berndtsson et al. (2008) apply one process, one proposal checklist, and one assessment-criteria list explicitly at both levels, grounding the Bachelor end in the ACM/IEEE CC2001 capstone note that research expectations of undergraduates must stay realistic. The survey read the book in full from a privately held copy; it is not part of this repository.

## The design consequence

Everything above supports the boundary the skills draw: one skeleton, one checker, the level read from the subtitle wording — the same inference path as language — and surfacing only as graded judgement. The guidelines' Degree Level section states four graded dimensions (contribution expectation, research-question origin, literature stance, scope-per-months), and five skills apply them at their natural moments: ideation steers the scope, writing types the contribution close and the research questions, review and the supervisor draft calibrate the bar in both directions, literature search weights its relevance judgement. Method justification is deliberately not among them: the frameworks' selection-and-justification outcomes describe the thesis and its grading, so a proposal declares one methodology and supports it as a whole — fit stays a review lens, never required prose. `structure.json`, the checker, and the document skeleton remain level-blind, and a programme that wants harder level-dependent rules (a Master-only reference floor, a different page limit per level) declares them in its workspace `guidelines.md`, per the customization surface — the sources themselves locate the difference in judgement, not structure, and jurisdictions weight it differently enough that anything stricter would stop being portable.
