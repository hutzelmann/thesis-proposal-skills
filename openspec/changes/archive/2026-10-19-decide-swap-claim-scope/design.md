## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- A pass of the swap test rests on text the proposal carries, never on what the judge, the conversation, the student's messages or an earlier review supplies.
- Mixed claim scope has one answer, and both the review and the supervise skill reach it from the text they ship.
- A supervise pass is evidence a verifier checks and the professor sees.
- An earlier round's pass, remark or demotion is checked again on the current text.
- The f31/f32 and f28 judgements do not move, and the setting-scoped Bachelor's control passes.

**Non-Goals:**
- No change to the adversarial check, the three-agent cap, the verifier's six-check remit or any execution-shape section.
- No pass record in `proposal-review`'s `review.md` (D6); a backlog entry.
- No verification of pass verdicts of the other blocking tests: only names have a procedure the text can be checked against. Out of scope for this report; a backlog entry.
- No widening of the guidelines' earlier-round class-climb exception beyond title names (maintainer decision 2026-10-19, D7).
- No change to `proposal-check`'s behaviour: its title wording stops asserting that a named technology is "correct as written", and the swap test stays the review's.
- No `check.py` rule, no `structure.json` key, no mechanical scope detector.
- No edit to oracles behind a shipped matrix cell (f04, f21) or a dev-runner L2 criterion (f31, f32); their audit is recorded in their README rows. f02, a comparison of unnamed tools for JavaScript, gains a `semantic` line.
- No Inspect task or matrix cell; no metered run.

## Decisions

**D1 — Mixed scope is undecided unless argued (maintainer decision 2026-10-18).** Where the thesis's own claims put the gap or the result on the class and others on the named instance, the instance is the claim's object only when the proposal states why the result is about it, as one of the dependences the guidelines list; otherwise, unless another anchor the proposal states holds (a constraint shaping the method, data or access no alternative offers, an argued case), the scope is undecided and the swap test fails. The rule decides the object-of-study anchor only: a stated constraint or an argued case still passes beside class-scoped claims, which keeps "Instrument that passes the swap test" and "Argued typical case" true. That the instance is at hand, or is what the student's organisation or team runs, is no reason. The repairs decide the scope: scope every gap to the named instance, say why the result is about it, or generalize the claim to the class. The rule is level-independent. *Alternatives put to the maintainer:* a Bachelor's-only all-or-nothing rule, under which an f32-shaped text would fail at Bachelor's level and pass at Master's, inverting the level bar the 2026-10-13 design built; and today's reading, under which the reported first verdict was correct and mixed scope is at most a refinement. *Trade-off:* a Bachelor's proposal that states a class-wide gap and applies in its setting without saying why that setting now gets a question (R1).

**D2 — The dependence rule is one sentence in the guidelines, after the anchor list.** "When a proposal is judged, a dependence counts once the proposal states it — that the research needs the name, not only that the name is at hand: a reason given only in conversation or in the student's messages, a fact about the setting the proposal leaves unstated or only implies, an earlier round's remark and an argument the reviewer supplies from what it knows pass nothing, and a sentence saying only that the method or evaluation uses the name — builds on its services, runs in its environment, compares against its logs — states use, not dependence. For the object of study the claims themselves state it: when every claim listed below is scoped to the instance, the scope is decided, and no reason for the name is asked, in a blocker or a refinement." "An earlier round's remark" rather than "an earlier review's": a professor-requested redo makes an earlier run's feedback an earlier round too (D7). "No reason … is asked, in a blocker or a refinement" replaced "nothing more is asked" after measurement round 3, where sonnet's review of the consistently scoped f34 added refinements inviting a novelty claim and a generalization; the claim-list bounds that answered the round-1 overblocking of f34 came earlier (Measurements). It binds all four anchors at once, closes the "access at hand" reading of the data-or-access anchor without rewording it, and keeps the scope-only object-of-study pass that a consistently setting-scoped Bachelor's proposal makes. *Why "the student's messages" and not "the notes file":* supervise treats a work plan moved into the notes as the proposal's own text, so the notes as a whole cannot be excluded. *Alternative considered:* rewording each anchor ("the source of data or access the proposal shows no alternative could supply"). Rejected: four local edits for one rule, in a paragraph that is already long. *Why the guidelines:* supervise reads only the guidelines, and write's substance gate defers to "the guidance's swap test" (`skill-write` "Substance gate"). Ideate's count of a spoken anchor for coverage is not a swap-test pass, hence "When a proposal is judged". The Title section's student-facing sentence ("A reason you give in conversation counts once the proposal carries it") stays: it tells the student what to do, the new one tells the judge what counts.

**D3 — The claim list is a reading step, and every stated gap is on it (maintainer decision 2026-10-19).** Before the replacement: list the thesis's own claims, whether or not they carry the name, each with its scope (the named instance or setting, or its class); every gap the contribution section states is among them, at its opening as in its close, including a limitation attributed to cited work. Reports of what particular cited studies did are not claims. "Whether or not they carry the name" is load-bearing: the reported class-scoped sentences did not name the platform. *Alternative put to the maintainer:* list only a gap the proposal presents as the one the thesis fills, treating a cited-work limitation as state of the art; rejected because a lenient judge could relabel the reported opening sentences that way and judge only the close. The list stays judgement under the formalization boundary and the "no mechanical rule SHALL try to tell a decorative name from an anchored one" clause.

**D4 — Supervise points at the guidelines, not at the review skill.** Both "exactly as the review skill would/does" pointers in Find the issues and "classified as the review skill does" in the review paragraph name the guidelines instead. The named-tools sentence becomes: "Named tools, systems, platforms and domains in the thesis's own claims are held to the swap test as the guidelines' Substance Tests section states it. Before you write a verdict on any name, reread its paragraph on names: list the thesis's own claims with their scope, the class-scoped ones without the name and every gap the contribution section states included — a limitation it attributes to cited work too — and the introduction's motivation and reports of what particular cited studies did left out; make the replacement; credit only a dependence the proposal states; and fail a name whose claims put the gap or the result on its class and on itself, unless the proposal says why the result is about it or states another anchor." Pinned as `proposal-supervise--swap-guidelines.txt`; after the final review its claim-list clause reads as the review's does — "the class-scoped ones without the name and every gap the contribution section states included — a limitation it attributes to cited work too — and the introduction's motivation and reports of what particular cited studies did left out" — since the shorter "reports of cited work left out" invited the relabelling D3 rejects. *Alternative considered:* an explicit `../proposal-review/SKILL.md` read with a fallback. Rejected: the swap test is core function, `skill-packaging` requires synchronized copies for core assets, and loading the review skill would run its workflow too. The reread is tied to writing a verdict, the step the reporting run took on its early read.

**D5 — A supervise pass is verified through the ledger, under fidelity.** A passing name enters the ledger as a claim about the proposal: the scope of each claim the test covers; the comparable alternative put in its place with the quoted claim that would not stand with it ("comparison by design" for a comparison of named tools); and the text that carries the pass, quoted — for the object of study the instance-scoped claims, the gap among them; for another anchor the sentence stating it. A pass no quoted text carries does not stand. The verifier's check 1 gains one bullet that names the claims the swap test covers, because the verifier reads no guidelines (wording drafts). A refuted entry sends the name back to the swap test on the corrected entry; only a failure there makes it a blocker, and the points and the tier are decided again, then verified. The review's verification section lists each pass with the text that carries it and any verifier dispute with the decision taken. *Alternatives considered:* a seventh verifier check, which contradicts the pinned "its six checks" and the fixed remit in "Single-context execution" (4154, cannot grow); widening the adversarial check to passes, which is pinned, workflow-only and absent on the reported host; handing `review.md` to the verifier, which makes it a second reviewer; making every refuted pass a blocker, which would block f32-shaped text whose list merely missed a class claim. *Why fidelity is enough:* the bug was a dependence credited to the proposal that the proposal does not state and an omitted class-scoped claim, both fidelity errors. The verifier applies no guideline rule; the supervising agent re-applies the rule to the corrected entry.

**D6 — Review: unconditional replacement, scope-decided anchor, no pass record.** The named-tools bullet keeps the anchors first (the 2026-10-13 D3 guard against overblocking), glosses the object of study as "the expected contribution claims its result for this instance, its scope decided", and drops "Otherwise": "… a comparison of named tools passes by design. A dependence counts only as the proposal states it (the guidelines' Substance Tests). For every name, an anchored one included, list the thesis's own claims with their scope — every gap the contribution section states kept, the introduction's motivation and reports of what particular cited studies did left out — then replace it with a comparable one, or drop it — does the same claim still stand, with only the noun changed? Then the name is decorative, unless every claim you listed, those without the name included, is scoped to it — the object of study, with nothing more asked — or an anchor the proposal states holds and no claim leaves the scope undecided; a passing name gets no swap-test finding." The scope-only escape ("unless every claim you listed … is scoped to it") was added after measurement round 3, where haiku failed f34 on the replacement twice although every claim names Mendwright: with the replacement made unconditional, a reader who stops at "the same claim still stands, with only the noun changed" fails every object of study. The blocker's content follows the guidelines' one-finding rule: where the scope is undecided it quotes a class-scoped and an instance-scoped claim and adds "scope every gap to the name" to the repairs. After the final review, the blocker also says "that the proposal has not decided which scope its result holds for", as the guidelines ask; the round-4 haiku review of f33 had left it out. The pinned clause stays byte-identical. The method exemption becomes "tools named only in the method". The title bullet exempts "a named technology that passes the swap test (next item) as the object of study", and the level-fit bullet's "complete at Bachelor's level" gains "its named setting still held to the swap test". `proposal-check`'s title bullet drops "is correct as written and". *Alternative considered:* a `## Names held to the swap test` record in `review.md`. Deferred to the backlog: it needs "A name that passes SHALL NOT be reported under the swap test" modified inside a 2020-character requirement, an L1 exclusion helper like `without_rq_record`, and a decision on the L2 criterion that checks the title name is reported once. Supervise, whose review the professor reads, gets the record (D5).

**D7 — Earlier rounds: title-only class climb, passes judged again, redo read as an earlier round (maintainer decisions 2026-10-19).** Supervise's monotone sentence carries the guidelines' exception verbatim — a title name the earlier round reported only as a title refinement, without holding it to the swap test, returns as a point recorded as missed in the earlier round when it fails that test — and adds that an earlier pass of a name, or a remark that it is the object of study, is no finding: the name is judged on the current text, and a failure is a new point recorded as missed in the earlier round. An earlier point withdrawn or demoted on unchanged text is recorded under Earlier round with its reason; a swap-test one is a pass, so it enters the ledger as one (D5) and meets the verifier. The record covers every earlier point, not only swap-test ones, because a silent withdrawal is what the reported redo did and a recorded reason costs one line; only the swap-test case has a ledger entry, since only names have a procedure the verifier can check (Non-Goals). A re-run the professor asks for over an earlier run's review or feedback for the same idea is an earlier round for the review — its points carried with their status, also when its files are replaced — while the feedback is written as for a first intake: no sentence on what a revision addressed and no point marked new, because the student received nothing new to revise against. The guidelines' "Rounds converge" and "Readiness classification" are not edited. *Alternatives put to the maintainer:* generalizing the class climb to any name an earlier round did not report as a swap-test failure, in review and supervise alike; and treating a redo as a replacement, or fully as a resubmission. *Edge left open:* a wrong pass recorded as a refinement on a non-title name stays shielded on unchanged text; the same round's ledger entry and verifier are meant to catch it before it is recorded.

**D8 — Quotes keep their qualifiers.** "Quote the student exactly; no ellipsis drops words the point then asks about, and a term cut from its sentence keeps the qualifier that sentence gives it wherever a point or a scope entry rests on it", in supervise and in the brief's check 1. The reported overshoot cited a term from the purpose statement as a class-level claim, without the platform qualifier its sentence gave it. The second verifier caught it, so this is a small sharpening, not a fix of a broken mechanism.

**D9 — Fixtures come from the swap pair's text.** f34-named-setting and f33-mixed-scope reuse f31's slug and text (`ci-repair-agent-configurations`) with the subtitle `*Bachelor's Thesis Proposal*` and the plan cut to Bachelor's scale (two RQs, one model family, the two architectures under one log condition, a per-category subsample), so no scope blocker clouds the verdict. Mendwright stands in the title, the purpose, the research focus and RQ1; RQ2 refers back to "either Mendwright configuration", so no research question reads as class-scoped. f31's reports of cited work become two neutral reports of what the studies did ("Repair studies report how often language-model agents fix failing builds and how good their repairs are", "Design studies compare single-agent and planner–executor architectures and measure how agents use long build logs"), and its class-level limitation, gap and significance are replaced in f34 by a gap scoped to Mendwright ("None of these studies has measured how Mendwright's own two architectures differ on build repair with the model held fixed"), a close "for Mendwright" and a significance about choosing between Mendwright's architectures, so f34 carries no class-scoped claim. f31's method sentence taking both architectures from Mendwright stays, one evaluation sentence says every run executes in Mendwright's sandbox, whose logs record each attempt — use, with no constraint stated — and the acceptance rule requires the project's other tests to pass in a fresh container, citing overfitting to the failing test. *Fixture revisions during measurement:* the first f34 kept f31's reports of cited work, whose wording implied a class-wide limitation, and an overfitting sentence the acceptance rule contradicted; opus and sonnet both read a class-scoped claim into them (round 2), so they were rewritten as above. After round 7 the title became "Agent Architectures", since the plan varies the architecture only, and the context studies moved from the contribution section to the method sentence on the truncated log excerpt, the claim they support. After round 8 the design-studies sentence carries them again ("Design studies compare single-agent and planner–executor architectures [@Moreau25Planner] and measure how agents use long build logs [@Sato26Logs; @Rossi26Context]"), because a cluster of one publication breaks the guidelines' cluster rule; the method sentence keeps its citation. Both carry one bait sentence in the introduction: the thesis is written in a team whose CI pipeline already runs Mendwright; the benchmark is public, so no access argument holds. f33 is f34 plus two plain class-scoped gap sentences at the start of the contribution section that do not carry the name, with no reason why Mendwright. ## Risks / Trade-offs

- [R1 Overblocking a common Bachelor's shape: a class-wide gap in the contribution section, then an application in one setting] → Accepted by the maintainer (D1, D3). The repair is one sentence (the gap scoped to the setting, or why this setting), and the feedback offers the three directions that decide the scope. f34 and f32 guard the passing shapes on every model run. A domain or gap sentence that only motivates the topic in the introduction stays outside the test.
- [R2 The verifier disputes passes it cannot judge] → Ledger entries are claims with quotes; the brief names the covered claims; the verifier never applies the scope rule, and a refuted entry is judged again, not blocked outright.
- [R3 More text in a long paragraph that every judging skill reads] → Estimated at about 200 words in the guidelines, 200 in supervise and 60 in review; shipped: the swap paragraph from 349 to 725 words (the guidelines +390), supervise from 5,651 to 6,119 (+468, about 800 words left under the body cap of twice the median skill), review from 3,800 to 3,951 (+151). The measurement rounds added most of the difference, each addition answering a recorded finding.
- [R4 Opus behaves differently from sonnet on the new text] → Opus is in the measurement set because the report came from it; its served ID and CLI version go into the record.
- [R5 The reproduction does not reproduce] → Recorded as inconclusive under the changed conditions (D12); the fixtures and verdicts still guard the rule.
- [R6 Matrix questions move] → f21 and f04 back shipped matrix cells; their oracles are not edited, and the new rule does not change f21's verdict (its title is its only instance-scoped claim).
- [R7 Concurrent work on develop] → `host-neutral-script-paths` (in flight) edited supervise's script-path lines; this change touches other lines and no shared requirement. Implement on a branch from current develop and re-run the sync after any merge touching `shared/`.
