## Why

The thesis title is the one part of a proposal that outlives the document: it is printed on the student's final study certificate. The guidance currently says nothing about it. Every "title" rule in `structure.json` and `guidelines.md` governs *section* headings; the proposal's own `title:` key is checked for presence and nothing else. Students therefore land tool-shaped and implementation-shaped titles ("Implementing an AI-Powered Kubernetes Dashboard") that read as a work order on a certificate and go stale the moment the tool does, and no skill says a word about it.

## What Changes

- `guidelines.md` gains the title rule as prose: the title names what is contributed and what it is about, at an abstraction that stays true if the tool is replaced. Concrete technology may appear only as a scope qualifier, and only once the student has stated why the tool is the object of study rather than the instrument.
- Four alarm classes are named: a tool, product, or vendor name; implementation framing over contribution; vague or grandiose field-naming; marketing or clickbait tone.
- The alarm is **raise-and-justify**, never a silent block: the agent always says it, offers abstracted alternatives, and keeps a named technology only against a stated justification.
- `structure.json` gains a `title` block holding only the mechanically matchable tells: bilingual implementation-opener patterns, a short bilingual buzzword list, and word-count bounds. Tool-specificity stays agent judgment — the set is unbounded and cannot be listed.
- `check.py` gains four warning-class checks over the `title:` value. Warnings, not errors: the heuristics can false-positive on a legitimate title, and the script cannot judge whether a named tool is the object of study.
- `proposal-ideate` treats the working title as working: it raises the alarm and offers alternatives, states plainly that the final title reaches the certificate, and does not force a final title before the research questions exist.
- `proposal-write` runs the binding negotiation once the research questions exist, including on a title inherited from a seed.
- `proposal-check` judges the classes no pattern reaches during its agent pass.
- `proposal-review` assesses the title as its own dimension.
- Verification: recalibrated fixture oracles, a dedicated bad-title fixture, unit tests for the new checks, and one metered L2 eval covering the agent-judgment half that no offline test can reach.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

_None._

### Modified Capabilities

- `guidance-model`: adds the thesis title as a governed part of the proposal, with the positive criterion, the four alarm classes, and the raise-and-justify posture; extends the formalization boundary to state which title tells are data and which stay prose.
- `skill-check`: adds title tells to the warning-class pattern checks and title judgment to the agent pass.
- `skill-write`: adds the binding title negotiation once research questions exist.
- `skill-ideate`: adds the working-title alarm without forcing a final title.
- `skill-review`: adds the title as an assessed dimension.
- `testing-harness`: adds the bad-title fixture and the L2 title-alarm eval task.

## Impact

- `shared/guidelines/guidelines.md`, `shared/structure.json` — authority and data, with generated copies regenerated by `scripts/sync_shared.py`.
- `skills/proposal-check/scripts/check.py` — new warning checks (vendored copies in `proposal-import` and `proposal-write` re-synced).
- `skills/proposal-{ideate,write,check,review}/SKILL.md` — skill behavior.
- `tests/fixtures/` — one new fixture plus recalibrated `expected.json` oracles wherever the new warnings fire.
- `tests/unit/` — direct unit tests for the title checks and the structure/prose drift guard.
- `harness/skill_evals.py` — one new metered L2 task.
- Untouched: `proposal-import`, `proposal-publish`, `proposal-customize`, `proposal-lit-search`.
