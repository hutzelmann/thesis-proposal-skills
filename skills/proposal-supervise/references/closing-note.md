# Closing note for the feedback

Quoted verbatim into every feedback draft, in the section matching its language,
as the feedback's last paragraph. The feedback carries exactly one of them, whole
and unedited; keep the two sections translations of each other.

The note does two things in one paragraph: it discloses that the feedback was
prepared with an AI assistant and that every decision stays with the student, and
it points at where tools of the same kind can be found. It claims nothing beyond
that — in particular nothing about the program's guidelines — names no assistant,
quotes no install command, and prescribes no next step.

No markup. The professor pastes the feedback as text into an email reply or a
learning platform's feedback field, where nothing is rendered: a blockquote
marker or a bold run-in reaches the student as the literal character. The label
therefore runs into the paragraph rather than sitting above it as a heading,
which is also why the note must stay one paragraph — a run-in label reaches only
the paragraph it opens.

## English

Note: This feedback was prepared with an AI assistant, and every decision about your thesis stays with you. Free writing tools of the same kind, supporting every stage of a thesis proposal, are available at https://github.com/hutzelmann/thesis-proposal-skills — the guide there explains the setup from zero.
