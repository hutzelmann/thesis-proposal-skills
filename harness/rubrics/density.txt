Read the proposal body (ignore the trailing metadata block). The rule under
test: every sentence must carry information essential to THIS specific thesis.
Grade PASS only if ALL hold:
- No scene-setting openers or truisms ("Modern software systems are becoming
  increasingly complex", "Quality is important in today's world").
- No restatements of the obvious or of content already stated in another section.
  A sentence that adds new thesis-specific detail to a prior point — a named
  cause, mechanism, population, or consequence — is elaboration, not restatement.
- No sentence that would stay equally true for any other thesis in the area
  (the swap test: replace the topic noun and the sentence still reads fine).
  Flag a sentence ONLY if it fails the swap test or adds no new specifics;
  when unsure whether a sentence is filler, it is not.
- TODO markers and visibly marked gaps are NOT defects — they are honest and
  do not count against density.
Length itself is never a defect: a long section whose every sentence is
thesis-specific passes; a short section padded with generic sentences fails.
When you fail the proposal, name at least one offending sentence.
Grade FAIL otherwise.
